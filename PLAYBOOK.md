# Cold Outreach Playbook / SOP

Operator checklist, built only from claims-table rows with a real link, real date, and verbatim quote. Follow section order top to bottom: don't invest in later steps until earlier ones are locked.

## 1. List building & targeting

1. Do not polish copy until the offer and the list are confirmed right — nothing else matters until then (source: AJ Cassata, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8UURle3hy0 from 24.11.2025).
2. Segment large prospect lists by role, industry, or company size rather than running one blanket campaign — it's more work but worth it (source: AJ Cassata, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2SuyVmT6GcY from 29.12.2025).
3. Also segment by department and seniority level, not narrow job titles alone (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YZg1uBWN2pA from 05.10.2022).
4. Build a narrowly targeted list around the specific problems of one ICP segment instead of broad company-size ranges — narrower targeting also makes the copy easier to write (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).
5. Target on a specific relevance signal (e.g., following a competitor's or tool's account) rather than generic personalization (source: Daniel Fazio, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5TV35VnArAQ from 15.12.2022).
6. Validate every lead list through an external SMTP validation tool, even when the source claims it's already verified (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
7. Recycle (re-contact) a small-TAM list roughly every 3 months; a large-TAM list every 6-12 months (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025).

## 2. Deliverability & infrastructure

**Domains and mailboxes**
1. Never send cold email from your primary/main business domain — buy secondary domains and forward replies back to the primary (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025; convergent with Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8gBcbQcNvJo from 03.03.2023).
2. Verify any infrastructure reseller is a legitimate, licensed reseller before buying — avoid resellers running edu/nonprofit domain panels (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
3. Let a new domain/tenant sit for about 5 days before creating inboxes and starting warmup (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
4. Warm new sending accounts for about 14 days, ramping up from ~5 emails/day rather than starting at full volume (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8gBcbQcNvJo from 03.03.2023).
5. Start infrastructure at roughly 10 domains / 30 mailboxes (3 senders per domain), 30-50 emails/day per mailbox; don't scale past that until current campaigns are showing good results (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025).
6. Don't exceed roughly 1,500 emails/day total per Microsoft tenant (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
7. Keep total per-inbox volume (cold + warmup combined) around 50-100 emails/day — Abraham's range tops out near 50-60, Bay's near 50-100 (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YZg1uBWN2pA from 05.10.2022; Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
8. Diversify sending infrastructure across multiple domains/inboxes so one domain landing in spam doesn't take down the whole pipeline (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).

**Content and monitoring**
9. Include no links or tracking links of any kind in the first cold email; only add links (video, Loom, Calendly) starting the second or third email (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025).
10. Avoid links or media (video/images/gifs) in the first email altogether, and never link to a domain with a low trust score (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025; Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025).
11. Turn off click tracking and avoid spam-trigger words such as "free" or dollar signs (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
12. Vary subject lines with spin-tax elements, and spin-tax at minimum the greeting and closing/signature, so each send looks different to ESPs (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8gBcbQcNvJo from 03.03.2023; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
13. Don't send to unverified addresses — keep bounce rate under 5% (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
14. When deliverability problems appear, check the sending domain with a blacklist checker (e.g., MX Toolbox) (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025).
15. If deliverability is measurably worse for a specific provider (e.g., Outlook), pause emailing those prospects there and prioritize LinkedIn or calling instead (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).

## 3. Sequence structure

1. Keep a prospecting sequence to 5-7 steps including automated follow-ups (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021).
2. Send no more than about 2 follow-up emails per week to the same prospect, spread over 3-4 weeks, then stop (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
3. Only introduce links (YouTube, Loom, Calendly) from the second or third email onward, never the first (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025).
4. Combine LinkedIn connection/message, cold email, and phone follow-up in the same sequence so you reach the prospect regardless of their channel preference (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021).

## 4. Copywriting

**Before you write**
1. Have a human review and edit any AI-drafted outreach copy — don't fully outsource copywriting to AI (source: AJ Cassata, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2SuyVmT6GcY from 29.12.2025).
2. Mine prospects' actual replies for their exact wording and reuse that precise language in your copy (source: Daniel Fazio, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5TV35VnArAQ from 15.12.2022).

**Subject line**
3. Keep it to five words or fewer (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
4. Make it about the prospect/their problem, not your company, and don't let it signal that a pitch is coming (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025; Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025).
5. Make it trigger curiosity, stay simple, and avoid spammy phrases (source: Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024).
6. Ground it in something specific and true about the recipient — all cold emails need to start with context (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwxpLOTm2A0 from 12.10.2023).

**Opening**
7. Don't open with "Hi [name], hope you're doing well" — it reads as a spam trigger; use an interrupt-pattern opener and delay the first name to the second sentence (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025; Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNLf2f1jwgo from 05.09.2020).
8. Open with a specific observation about the recipient rather than generic content (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).

**Body / offer framing**
9. Write focused on the prospect's situation and what they can do as a result — not on your product, your credentials, or a description of your service (source: AJ Cassata, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8UURle3hy0 from 24.11.2025; Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024; Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aFBXKS3cbJU from 15.03.2023).
10. Frame around the outcome for the client, not what you do (source: AJ Cassata, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OaQXAIQwuvU from 21.04.2023).
11. Be specific about what you offer and how it helps — never vague ("we offer great services") (source: Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024).
12. Replace vague percentage claims with a specific number plus a timeframe (e.g., "$32,000 generated in 47 days") (source: Daniel Fazio, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t1QEt7JNx5E from 26.05.2023).
13. Diagnose what solution the prospect already uses before pitching — don't sell "a better bucket" to someone who already has a bucket (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwxpLOTm2A0 from 12.10.2023).
14. Never make self-praising, unverifiable claims ("we're the best") or hypey, too-good-to-be-true claims ("if I could 10x your revenue...") — both trigger resistance (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cKZVyAQ3mY from 17.04.2024).
15. Don't rely on a generic "customer satisfaction" guarantee as your differentiator (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CMfMrZaOxrc from 18.06.2026).
16. Structure the email on the 4T framework — Trigger, Think, Third-party validation, then one crisp "how it works" sentence — and make sure the trigger sentence logically ties to the question that follows; use third-party proof as a numeric contrast (e.g., "94% vs 12%"), not self-praise (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cKZVyAQ3mY from 17.04.2024).
17. Write to win three sequential gates in order — get it opened, get it read as interesting, then get a reply (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cANfDSKS_ps from 22.03.2024; Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025).
18. De-risk the offer with pay-only-when-they-get-paid pricing to lift response rates (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aFBXKS3cbJU from 15.03.2023).
19. Keep the core message short up top; put detailed case-study/process context below the signature so it doesn't add friction but is there for credibility (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iRxyDhQ6qDA from 15.11.2024).
20. Add a PS line offering to redirect to the right person ("should I be reaching out to this person or this person instead") to lift replies (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iRxyDhQ6qDA from 15.11.2024).

**Length, format, CTA**
21. Keep cold emails to around 75 words, formatted for mobile with short 1-2 sentence paragraphs — prospects skim rather than read every word (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025; Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).
22. Limit each email to one core stat/number rather than stacking several (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
23. Give a single, direct, easy-to-follow CTA that tells the recipient exactly what to do next (source: Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024).
24. Read the email out loud before sending — if it sounds cringey or forced, don't send it (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).

## 5. Personalization

1. Base the intro/icebreaker line on genuine specific research (e.g., a podcast episode you actually engaged with), not generic personalization (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://mixergy.com/interviews/lemlist-with-guillaume-moubeche/ from 14.06.2021).
2. Reference a concrete intent signal in the copy (e.g., "I saw you're following [company] on LinkedIn") (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
3. Personalize with the recipient's name plus a specific detail about their business (source: Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024).
4. If a personalized detail (company name, title) can't be formatted naturally, leave it out — an awkward insert flags the email as mass cold email (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024).
5. Keep AI-driven personalization tightly aligned with the actual offer — don't insert irrelevant personalized details just to appear personalized (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).
6. Segment prospects into 3 value tiers and personalize proportionally more for the highest-value tier (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNLf2f1jwgo from 05.09.2020).
7. If using outreach video, keep it under a minute — and don't bother building dynamic per-recipient video personalization, it didn't outperform standardized videos (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNLf2f1jwgo from 05.09.2020; https://mixergy.com/interviews/lemlist-with-guillaume-moubeche/ from 14.06.2021).

## 6. Follow-ups & handling replies

1. Make the first follow-up short and quick — just checking the original email was received — then run 5-6 more follow-ups spaced 6-8 days apart (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNLf2f1jwgo from 05.09.2020).
2. Include voicemails in the cadence — they roughly double email reply rate; point the prospect to a specific email rather than asking for a callback (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025).
3. Keep following up until you get a clear yes, a no, or get blocked (source: Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024).
4. If a prospect who said they'd follow through goes quiet, re-engage with a line like "have you given up on [X]?" (source: Josh Braun, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f3EnXR13xxI from 03.01.2024).
5. After a few unanswered touchpoints, escalate to another channel (LinkedIn message, then a call) instead of giving up (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).
6. On any interested reply: call immediately rather than replying only by email, and prioritize calling leads who already replied over dialing net-new leads (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024; Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CMfMrZaOxrc from 18.06.2026).
7. Don't send a copy-pasted templated reply to a positive responder — personalize the response, and don't info-dump pricing/features — hold details back for the call (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwt7AgpnPXE from 30.04.2025).
8. When a lead engages (e.g., clicks) but doesn't book, reply as fast as possible — automate the reply where you can (source: Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).
9. Keep cold and warm leads in separate CRMs; only move a lead to the warm CRM once they take a real action (book a call, opt in), not just express interest (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025).
10. Never end a call — cold call or follow-up call — without locking in a specific next step on the calendar (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAz7BegV-RE from 01.07.2025; Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024).

## 7. Metrics & optimization

1. Target at least a 1% positive reply rate before scaling volume further (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025).
2. Treat a 5% reply rate as an excellent campaign and 2% as good enough to keep running; below that, change the lead magnet or targeting (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024).
3. Aim for a 1-2% opportunity (qualified positive reply) rate (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024).
4. Aim for above 5% overall response rate as a general campaign benchmark (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021).
5. Target at least a 50% open rate as a baseline, rising to above 60% once volume reaches 1,000 emails/day (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024).
6. Expect roughly 28-70% open rate on a healthy campaign, and treat a sub-50% open rate as a deliverability problem, not a subject-line problem (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021).

## Where experts disagree

1. **CTA style: open-ended ask vs. specific limited choice.**
Nick Abraham says his lowest-performing CTA is pushing straight to a meeting, and that an open-ended, interest-based question outperforms it (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8gBcbQcNvJo from 03.03.2023; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YZg1uBWN2pA from 05.10.2022). Jason Bay agrees that interest-based CTAs have the highest success rate (source: Jason Bay, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcJtlxDub2E from 23.01.2025) — but in the same talk also says to avoid open-ended CTA questions altogether, a direct internal contradiction. Guillaume Moubeche takes the opposite position from Abraham: give a specific limited choice ("Are you available Tuesday at 3pm?") rather than an open-ended availability ask (source: Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021).

I'm leaving this unresolved rather than picking a side. Bay contradicting himself in the same source is a signal that "open-ended vs. specific" isn't the variable actually driving the result — something upstream (offer strength, list warmth, channel) is probably doing the real work, and both camps may be right under different conditions. Forcing a single CTA rule into the SOP would manufacture confidence the data doesn't support.

2. **Whether the first email can ask for anything at all.**
Jay Feldman says not to ask for anything — not a call, not a download, not a purchase — in the first cold email; lead with free value only (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024). That's in tension with every other CTA recommendation in this playbook, all of which assume the first email carries some kind of ask: Alex Berman's "single, direct, easy-to-follow CTA" (source: Alex Berman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hatrtJIncLc from 03.09.2024), and Abraham's and Bay's CTA-phrasing advice above.

Also left unresolved, but I don't think it's a clean disagreement so much as an underspecified one. Feldman's claim reads as an argument for a specific sequence architecture — value-first email, ask deferred to email two or three — not a blanket claim that asking is always wrong. Whether that's compatible with the other experts' advice depends on how "the first email" is defined once a lead-magnet step is in play, and the sourced material doesn't say enough to adjudicate it either way.

3. **Whether to track open rate at all. (Resolved)**
Nick Abraham and Xavier Caffrey both say to turn off open tracking entirely, arguing the tracking pixel itself is what damages deliverability (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024; Xavier Caffrey, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgCRhgbfEF0 from 22.10.2024). Jay Feldman and Guillaume Moubeche both give explicit open-rate benchmarks and treat a low open rate as a diagnostic signal, which only works if tracking is on (source: Jay Feldman, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZeF9OdC-fk from 17.01.2025; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ito99oYGmAY from 07.02.2024; Guillaume Moubeche, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QsaBaT2-DM from 25.04.2021).

I'm siding with Feldman and Moubeche and keeping the benchmarks in section 7. Reply rate alone is too slow and too coarse a signal to run a campaign on — opens accumulate in hours, replies take days, so open rate is the earliest warning something is wrong before a week of send volume is spent finding out from reply data. It also separates two failure modes that reply rate collapses into one number: a subject-line/targeting problem shows up as a low open rate on an otherwise fine campaign, while a body-copy/offer problem shows up as a decent open rate with no replies. Abraham and Caffrey's objection is real, but it's an argument for tracking carefully (plain text, no third-party pixel service riding on your domain reputation), not for flying blind. I'd rather manage that risk than give up a metric this diagnostically useful.

## What I rejected and why

1. **Guessing info@ addresses when a named contact can't be found.**
Nick Abraham suggests that when you can't find a person's email for a region, you can guess the generic info@domain address and still get a meaningful reply rate (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CMfMrZaOxrc from 18.06.2026). I left this out. The entire playbook is built on precision — narrow lists, real relevance signals, personalization that proves you did the work. A generic info@ inbox is the opposite: it's usually unmonitored or handled by someone with no authority, and it drags down deliverability with bounces and low engagement, which are the exact metrics this playbook protects. The small reply-rate bump isn't worth polluting a carefully warmed sending infrastructure. If I can't reach a real decision-maker, I'd rather switch channel (LinkedIn, call) than dilute the list.

2. **Spin-tax / deliverability gaming to make sends look unique.**
Nick Abraham recommends spinning subject lines and at least the greeting and signature so each send looks different to email providers (source: Nick Abraham, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8gBcbQcNvJo from 03.03.2023; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WWNcFm94xao from 25.11.2024). I rejected it as a core tactic. Spin-tax treats deliverability as a technical trick to outrun spam filters, but the durable version of deliverability in this playbook comes from relevance and low complaint rates — if the right people want the email, engagement signals keep you in the inbox. Spin-tax also actively fights the copywriting standard the rest of the playbook holds: Braun and Bay push for one tight, deliberate message, and rotating fragments to fool a filter degrades that craft. It's a patch for a volume problem I'm choosing not to have.

## Who I would NOT recommend following (and why)

Of the ten experts I researched, the one I'd hesitate to recommend — specifically as a source for building a sourced, verifiable playbook — is Maxim Tanevskiy. Across the two files I collected, I couldn't extract a single concrete, citable cold-outreach recommendation: no specific tactic tied to a real claim, nothing that survived into the claims table even as unsourced material. Every other expert produced usable, specific guidance; he was the sole outlier at zero.

I want to be precise about the claim, because it's easy to overstate. This isn't "his advice is wrong" — it's that, relative to the other nine and within the material I gathered, his content was the lowest-signal for this purpose: general or motivational rather than specific and actionable enough to cite. For a playbook where every line must trace to a dated, verifiable source, a voice that yields nothing extractable is the one I'd point a newcomer away from first.

A secondary, softer note on Alex Berman: his most distinctive ideas ("targeting beats copywriting," "Clay is a scaling tool, not a research tool") were compelling but lived in undated LinkedIn posts that couldn't clear a citation bar, while his citable material was comparatively generic. I'd still recommend him as a voice — but for a sourced playbook specifically, much of his best thinking wasn't usable.

## Weaknesses of this playbook

This playbook has real limitations I want to be honest about. First and most important: all ten experts I researched sell cold-email services, courses, or tools. Their advice is shaped by what they need to be true to sell — it carries built-in self-promotion and survivorship bias, and none of them publish the campaigns that failed. Second, the evidence base skews heavily toward long-form YouTube content, because that's what came with resolvable links and dates; the LinkedIn material, where several of these practitioners are sharpest, was largely undated and had to be excluded, so the playbook undersamples it. Third, almost none of the specific numbers here (open-rate targets, volume caps, reply-rate benchmarks) are independently verified — they're practitioner claims, not tested results, and my own campaign would need to confirm them. Finally, this is a synthesis of others' recommendations plus my judgment calls; it hasn't been run end-to-end, so the sequencing and the disagreement resolutions are reasoned bets, not proven outcomes.

## Coverage gaps
